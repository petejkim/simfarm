# SimFarm native preservation

Incremental reconstruction of the supplied 16-bit DOS SimFarm as a native TypeScript/React/Vite browser game, developed with Bun. No DOS runtime or x86 emulator is used. The current build supports land purchase, new fields, planting, crop treatments, growing, harvesting, storage and crop sales, with machinery, weather, calendar progression and converted MIDI music. Positive-value corn farming cycles on existing fields and newly purchased/fertilized land are verified in the browser. Native save/load is available; DOS-compatible export and many other original systems remain unfinished.

## Run locally

Requires your original game files at `../simfarm-dos`. The bootstrap downloads Bun 1.4.2 into `.tools/bun` on macOS or Linux. No global installation or separate Node/npm runtime is needed.

```sh
sh tools/setup-bun.sh
./tools/bun install --frozen-lockfile
./tools/bun run import
./tools/bun run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. Choose one of eight scenarios, locate a field to see its crop and work status, scroll or use arrow keys to explore, zoom, click tiles, browse 24 crops, and start music. To import from another directory: `./tools/bun run import /path/to/simfarm-dos`.

Farms start paused. Select a field, choose **Crop to plant**, and press **Plant crop**. Planting replaces the existing crop, consumes stored seed or purchases it with a delivery surcharge, and uses owned or automatically rented machinery according to the farm's saved settings. Use **Next day** to step or **Play** to run. The **Speed** slider adjusts pacing from **0.25× to 16×**; at the default **1×**, one real second advances one game day. **Pause** stops progression, and **Next day** always advances one day regardless of speed. Machinery completes two field passes and returns home; crops then age, change appearance and can expire. Work already underway must finish before another planting order. Saved crop plans run automatically each week, including planting, harvest and field care. Weekly boundaries finish outstanding field work. Manual planting rebuilds that field’s future crop plan; care orders record the current week when it is available. To open the planner, select a field on the map (or under **Locate a field**), scroll down the side panel, and expand **Crop plan · Field N**. Choose a year and week, select a **Plan action**, then press **Apply plan change**. Future weeks support planting, field care, harvest and clearing the remaining plan. Planting replaces the plan from the selected week onward. Crops with fixed annual harvest dates cannot have their harvest moved. To lock a growing crop’s current price, select the current week and **Lock crop price**. Planner changes are included in native saves.

To expand, choose **Map tool → Buy land**, click a plot, check its price and soil fertility, and press **Buy plot**. The tool switches to **New field** at the plot's corner. Enable **Automatically clear trees and fences** when needed, choose a crop and press **Create and plant field**. You can also select **New field** directly and click an 8×8 field's top-left corner on owned land. The preview and status explain blocked placements; fields need a one-tile gap. New land retains its original soil conditions, often too poor for a valuable crop without fertilization.

To add storage, choose **Map tool → Build**, choose a building, click its top-left corner, and press **Build**. Small and large crop storage hold one or four harvested lots; returning equipment can use the other four storage buildings. The map preview and status show placement problems and clearing costs. **Automatically clear trees** shares the farm's clearing setting. Construction costs, buildings, contents and accounts survive **Save farm** / **Load farm**. The same menu offers **Water pump** and **Wind-powered irrigation**. A pump needs nearby water; wind-powered irrigation supplies its lower-left outlet when wind reaches 9 mph. Connected original ditches alternate wet and dry passes while time advances, affecting field water and crop condition. Choose **Map tool → Irrigation** to dig ditches, install valves, and open or close them.

To clear your farm, choose **Map tool → Bulldoze**, click a field, building, loose supply, chemical or removable terrain, review the highlighted footprint, and press **Confirm removal · $25**. Field removal destroys its crop; building demolition destroys all stored contents without sale proceeds. Working fields must reach Waiting or Growing first. **Cancel selection** leaves the farm unchanged. Clearing continues while time advances, after which you can reuse the space. Loose supplies and chemicals lose their remaining contents. Terrain removal updates connected roads, fences, ditches and shoreline; town-owned tiles cannot be cleared. Terrain changes immediately, while removed objects use the clearing animation.

Select a field and use **Field care → Fertilizer → Apply fertilizer** to restore its nutrients. Pesticide, herbicide and fungicide remove current pests, weeds or fungus and reduce their pressure, but add soil toxicity. Treatments use one stored supply or automatically buy it for $275 according to the original shared supply-buying setting. A tractor and sprayer complete the job while time advances; missing machines may incur rental fees. Wait for planting/harvest work to finish before ordering treatment. Fertilizer does not undo damage already accumulated by the crop, so treating poor soil early matters.

Select **Harvest crop** to send machinery or workers to a growing crop. Maturity and estimated value help with timing; early or damaged crops can be worth $0. Harvests enter available storage, or sell automatically when storage is unavailable. Use **Sell for $…** under **Harvest in storage** to sell a stored lot and free its space. Worker fees and missing equipment rentals can reduce returns. Other fields also follow their saved plans, and planting changes live crop prices, including the sale value of stored lots.

Expand **Loans** directly below the time controls to borrow against your farm, see the installment quote, or **Pay off loan**. Installments run at the start of January, March, July and October; unpaid installments retry monthly and can trigger forced asset sales. The overdue installment is shown separately from the main loan balance. If available assets cannot cover a debt, the game stops and offers **Restart this farm** or **End session**. Loan balances and pending decisions survive native saves. Annual tax collection, town choices and development are active; expand **Taxes** and **Farm report** for their current figures.

Use **Save farm** to download a `.simfarm.json` checkpoint and **Load farm** to resume it, including after closing or reloading the page. Saves retain work in progress, weather, random state, prices, financial accounts, pending default decisions, pause and speed. Load requires the same imported original assets; invalid files leave the current farm intact. These are native saves, not DOS-compatible SSM files. Switching scenarios starts a fresh paused simulation at 1× speed, so save first to keep your work. Clock pacing is a browser convenience, not the recovered DOS interrupt rate. Seed/rental charges, loan installments, monthly debt retries, annual taxes/reset and town development run. Remaining disaster types, scenario-specific behavior and other documented systems remain incomplete.

Original files are never modified. Extracted resources are generated in ignored `public/assets`; they are not committed. Import precedes building so the browser bundle can run independently of the DOS distribution directory. Missing resources produce startup instructions.

## Validate

```sh
./tools/bun test
./tools/bun run build
PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH=.tools/playwright ./tools/bun run playwright install chromium
./tools/bun run test:browser
```

Browser checks cover all maps, source-exact crop/overlay pixels, field inspection, crop selection, zoom, actual audio output, stopping playback, pending-load cancellation, missing-asset recovery, time and speed controls, land purchases, new-field creation, planting/treatment commands, worker/machine harvesting, storage and complete plant/grow/harvest/sell cycles, including fertilized new land. See `docs/progress.md` for the latest completed validation and any environment limitations. Tests read the original source distribution. `./tools/bun run preview` serves the production build.

## Static reconstruction tools

```sh
sh tools/setup-analysis.sh
./tools/bun run analyze --start 0x4543c --size 0x1000
```

Capstone and its dependencies are installed only in `.venv`. The analyzer writes an overlay map and requested disassembly ranges to ignored `artifacts`. Bun downloads use `.cache/bun`; browser binaries use `.tools/playwright`. `tools/bun` sets repository-local tool/cache paths, and `bunfig.toml` runs script dependencies with Bun even when they declare Node shebangs. See [Bun's runtime configuration](https://bun.sh/docs/runtime/bunfig) and [Vite's React plugin support](https://vite.dev/guide/features.html).

React components in `src/ui` own menus, selections and lifecycle. `src/render` draws the native canvas, `src/formats` decodes original data, `src/assets` loads resources, and `src/audio` plays MIDI and original PCM effects. Recovered simulation belongs outside React so game rules can be tested independently and future graphics/UI changes can reuse them.

`src/simulation/GameSimulation` (implemented in `game.ts`) owns global game state, provides detached snapshots through `getSnapshot`/`subscribe`, and accepts commands. React uses `useSyncExternalStore`; hooks do not own simulation state. Recovered calendar, weather, field conditions, growth and expiry routines are tested against original data and executable-derived cases. Random streams are deterministic with explicit seeds; complete DOS startup/frame random consumption is not reproduced yet.

See [the plan](docs/plan.md), [progress](docs/progress.md), and [binary evidence](docs/reverse-engineering.md). The world decoder, crop graphics substitution, masked tile layer, moving machinery and field operations come from original executable evidence. Remaining render passes and game rules require more reconstruction. Music uses original MIDI notes/program changes with a temporary Web Audio synthesizer; instrument fidelity and XMIDI looping are unfinished.

At year-end your homestead changes with the original cash bands. Upgrade/downgrade notices include **View home** to center the map. **Play music** also enables automatic original track changes from farm conditions and home upgrades; **Stop music** keeps those requests silent. **Enable sounds** separately enables the original cheers/boos and horse, cow, pig and sheep effects. Both audio controls start off. Native saves retain the house and its last applied rank without replaying its sounds on load.

Open **Livestock** above the map for counts, ages, breeding progress and care notices, then **Buy animals and supplies** (also available under **Map tool → Animals & supplies**). Choose a horse, cow, pig, sheep, water trough or feed, select a map tile, then buy. Keep feed and water near your animals. Animals move, drink, eat, graze, use storage, mature, reproduce and eventually die of old age. Their ongoing state resumes after saving/loading. Troughs start with256 water units and feed with512 units. Move the pointer near an animal on the map to influence its direction when it is not seeking food/water. Enable sounds to hear original animal voices; muting does not change simulation behavior. Original audio arbitration and other fidelity gaps remain documented.

For automatic trough refills, choose **Map tool → Build → Water storage** and place it beside a wet ditch supplied by a pump or wind-powered irrigation. It collects water on odd days and supplies empty troughs anywhere on the farm each week. The Livestock panel shows stored water and worn-out locations; select a water store with the Build tool for its quantity and condition. Buildings and inventory now age weekly, including the original stored-crop/seed loss rules.

Open **Machinery** above the map to inspect owned/rented counts, wear and automatic sale notices. Time and travel wear machines; at maximum wear an available owned machine is sold using the original accounting, with a clearing effect and original sound when enabled. Stored chemicals also age using the original counters without reducing treatment strength. To replace or add machines, choose **Machinery → Buy machinery** or **Map tool → Buy machinery**, choose a machine, click clear ground or an equipment storage building, then press **Buy machinery**. Prices, clearing costs and placement problems appear before purchase. Bought machines join the owned fleet automatically and survive native saves. Use **Machinery → Sell machinery** to select an owned machine, review its quote and sell it; busy machines must finish their work, and rentals cannot be sold. **Livestock → Sell livestock** offers the corresponding controls for animals, including newborns and stored animals.

To move ground equipment, choose **Machinery → Move machinery** or **Map tool → Move machinery**, click a machine, press **Select machine**, click its destination on your land, then press **Move here**. Resume play or advance days to let it travel. Towed implements need a tractor and may use automatic rental. To access stored machinery, click its building and press **Open building** first; **Inspect** also exposes **Open building / Close building**. Moving machinery, towing and open interiors survive native saves. Aircraft use their flight controls.

To relocate seeds, harvested crops, chemicals, feed or troughs, choose **Map tool → Move supplies** (also available under **Livestock**). Click the supply, press **Select supply**, click owned clear grass or compatible storage, then **Move here**. Transfers are immediate and free; **Cancel selection** changes nothing. Open buildings to reveal stored crop supplies. Empty feed is discarded on transfer.

Open **Aircraft** above the map to buy an aircraft at an available airport, refuel, load pesticide/herbicide/fungicide, or repair wear. **Show on map** locates the selected plane. Changing the loading chemical sells that plane’s remaining payload; the loading choice is shared by all aircraft. Press **Fly aircraft** to open flight controls above the map. Turn, change altitude and spray using the buttons or the keys shown under **Flight help**. End over an airport to land; ending elsewhere crashes the plane. Farm work and the calendar stop during flight; pause, step and speed controls remain available. Native saves preserve the loading choice, aircraft records and a flight in progress; older saves migrate automatically.


Expand **Disasters** below **Machinery** to allow or disable new disasters, manually **Start tornado** or **Start locust swarm**, and locate an active effect. Disabling new disasters lets an existing event finish. Tornadoes damage crops and destroy buildings with their contents; locusts damage a field. High winds can remove nutrients from exposed fields; surrounding trees provide protection. July and weekly wind triggers run automatically. Use **Enable sounds** for the original effects. Native v13 saves preserve active disasters, clocks and random state; loading does not replay start sounds. Tornadoes continue during aircraft flight and can cause turbulence or a crash. Drought, flood, frost, crop circles and toxic-land events remain unfinished.
